The quickest thing to learn: the dock tucks itself away to the side when you leave it alone. Nudge the edge of the screen and it comes back. Drag it to the other side if it is covering something.

Writing on whatever is on screen

  • The pen button draws over anything – a website, a video, a photo under the visualiser.
  • Press it again to put the pen away and get the mouse back.
  • The ring of tools that appears has the ruler, protractor and compass in it.
  • Start a line just above the ruler's edge and it comes out perfectly straight.

Worksheets, slides and past papers

  • The folder button opens a PDF, PowerPoint or Word file to write on.
  • P pen, G highlighter, L line, S shapes, T text, E rubber, C compass, R ruler, A protractor, H swipe.
  • Ctrl+R turns the page a quarter turn. Your writing turns with it.
  • Save offers the file back in the type you opened – a PowerPoint stays a PowerPoint, with the text still editable.
  • In swipe mode, drag left and right to change slide, or tap to play the next animation.
  • Style changes the line or shape you have just drawn, and sets how the next one comes out: straight, dashed, dotted or hand‑drawn, how thick, what colour, and whether a shape is filled in. Draw it first, look at it on the board, then change your mind.
  • Every colour wheel opens the same picker, with the six colours you used last at the top.

Text boxes and pictures

  • Pick Text and click the page to start writing. Click an existing box to carry on from the end.
  • Drag a box to move it; the handle halfway down its right side changes how wide it is; the corner changes the size of the type.
  • The bar above the box does bold, italic, underline, strike through, five fonts and colour. Highlight part of the words to change just that part.
  • Ctrl+V drops a picture straight onto the page. Drag it about, use the corner to resize and the grip above it to turn it.

Question sheets

  • The ? button on the dock makes starters and problem‑solving sheets.
  • A fluency starter takes up to three topics and shares the questions between them.
  • Ask for an answers page and it prints on the back.

Keeping the lesson on track

  • The objectives button puts the lesson objectives along the bottom of the board, and ticks them off as you go.
  • The timer is readable from the back row. It keeps counting while you teach.
  • ShapeXplorer opens shapes, nets, areas and volumes to explore in front of the class.

If something goes wrong

  • Windows nagging about an unknown app? Right‑click the zip, Properties, tick Unblock, then extract it.
  • Dock in the way? Drag it to the other side, or let it tuck itself away.
  • Anything else, or an idea for something it should do – email me. I read all of it.
